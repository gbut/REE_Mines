What's mapped

Stimson Center — “Mining in Southeast Asia” sheet behind the Stimson mining dashboard (Planet Labs imagery). The sheet has – rows covering Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar and several mining methods. Only rows with Country = Myanmar and Metal = Rare Earth are shown (– rows, in Kachin and Shan). Laos rows, alluvial, heap-leach, gold, jade, manganese, tungsten and unknown rows are left out.

Myanmar Witness / Centre for Information Resilience, with Mizzima — public ArcGIS layer behind the “Rare Earth Mining Map” (– sites in –; with pit counts and the year each site was visible by).

The two layers overlap heavily: Stimson credits Myanmar Witness for many Kachin points, and of the – Myanmar Witness sites sit within 300 m of a Stimson point. Each detail card names the nearest point in the other layer, so you can compare them.

Live data status

    Each time the page opens, it fetches the latest Stimson sheet rows, the Myanmar Witness layer, the BVC Archive sheet and the Reports list. If a source can't be reached, the page uses the copy saved inside it. A Stimson site's ID is its row number in Stimson's sheet (S0634 is row 634), so it stays the same when Stimson edits that row or others. IDs move only if Stimson inserts or deletes rows above it; the change list below records when that happens.

    Caveats

    • Points mark mine sites, not their footprints. One site can have dozens of leach pools.
    • “Year” is the year in the source record: when the site first appears in imagery for Stimson, and the year it was built by for Myanmar Witness. It doesn't show whether a mine is active now.
    • Default satellite imagery (Esri) is often several years old. Switch to Sentinel-2 cloudless, or use the per-site Esri Wayback and Google links, for other dates.
    • Other mapped work, including Global Witness, Crisis Group and SHRF, is published as images or proprietary map styles, not open coordinates. Those findings are linked under Reports, BVC Archive and Areas but aren't plotted as points.
